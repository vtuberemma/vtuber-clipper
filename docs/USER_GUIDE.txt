VTuber Clipper  ♡  v2.2 BETA  - no Python install needed
==========================================================
1. Unzip the WHOLE folder anywhere (keep python\, app\, bin\ next to VTuberClipper.exe).
2. Double-click VTuberClipper.exe.   (Windows SmartScreen: More info -> Run anyway.)

THIS IS A BETA. It works and has been tested a lot, but it was tested on a Linux test machine, not on every Windows PC, and new things
can still have rough edges. The window title, the banner and the Settings footer say "beta" so you always know. If something looks wrong:
Settings -> About (or press F1) -> "Copy info for a bug report", then paste it into your message together with what you did.

WHAT'S NEW IN 2.2 (beta)   - smarter clip finding, 50 new things, and a bug hunt
  The first thing to know: Smart finding is ON by default (Shorts -> "Smart finding" tab). It changes WHICH moments get picked and how they are labelled.
  To get the old way of finding clips back, untick "Smart finding (recommended)". Everything else that is new is OFF until you switch it on.

  HOW "TRAINED" IS MEANT HERE  (please read - it is not what it sounds like)
    - VTuber Clipper has no neural network and cannot learn from videos by itself. "Training" the finder means two honest things:
      (1) I tuned rules - word lists, sound detectors and clip lengths - for the kinds of clips VTubers are known for: gameplay, reactions (laughing,
          screaming, jump scares), talking, storytime and singing. That tuning used published advice about short videos and descriptions of well-known
          VTuber moments. I could not download or watch real VTuber clips on my side, so the sound detectors were tested on SOUNDS I SYNTHESISED
          (a scream, a laugh, a sung melody, speech) and on a made-up 4-minute stream. They were NOT tested on real VTuber footage.
      (2) The app now learns from YOUR choices: clips you star or mark as posted (and clips you keep or delete in Review mode) teach it which kinds and
          lengths you like ("Learn from the clips I keep"). It needs at least 3 kept clips before it does anything.
    - The advice it follows comes from published articles and analyses about short videos. I have not been able to check those claims myself,
      so treat them as rules of thumb: open on the best moment, no "hey guys, welcome back", a new beat every few seconds, cut right after the payoff,
      about 18-30 s for reactions and gameplay, 30-50 s for stories.
    - Please check what it picks the first few times, and tell me which kind of clip it gets wrong - that is the fastest way to make it better.

  THE 50 NEW THINGS
  🧠 Finding the right moment  (Shorts -> Smart finding)
     1  Five kinds of moment - gameplay, reaction, talking, story, singing - each with its own rules. Every clip is labelled with its kind.
     2  Match the captions to the kind (pop-in words for gameplay, line captions for stories, karaoke for singing).
     3  Laughing is heard in the sound, not only read in the words ("hahaha" typed by the speech-to-text is not enough).
     4  Screaming / squealing is heard in the sound.
     5  Singing is heard in the sound (steady pitch, held notes) and the lyrics are shown as karaoke captions.
     6  Setup -> punchline: the clip starts at the setup and ends at the laugh.
     7  Story shape: stories are snapped to where the thought starts and finishes.
     8  Greeting skip: clips don't start on "hey guys, welcome back".
     9  A much bigger vocabulary of laughs, screams, fails, wins, rage, scares, cute moments and chaos (also in a few other languages' common words).
    10  Variety: a mix of kinds instead of five clips of the same thing.
    11  Smart length: reactions run short, stories run longer (inside your min / max).
    12  End on the payoff: cuts the tail after the punchline.
    13  Chat helps: a saved chat log (.json / .csv / .txt) makes busy-chat moments count for more.
    14  "Why this moment" in each clip's title-ideas file (what was heard, what was read).
    15  My taste: learns from the clips you keep (needs 3+).
    16  Title and hook ideas that fit the kind of clip.
  🎬 Picture, sound and captions   (Captions / Picture & voice / Logo & music tabs)
    17  A logo that hops to a new corner every few seconds.
    18  Slow-motion on the biggest moment.
    19  A freeze-frame beat before the punchline.
    20  Teaser: a one-second flash of the best moment at the very start.
    21  Big reaction words that pop up (LOL, EEEEK!, OOF, LET'S GO!, NOPE...).
    22  Tame loud screams (a gentle limiter, so nobody's ears hurt).
    23  Hush hiss (removes steady background noise).
    24  Caption letter case: automatic / lower / normal / UPPER.
    25  Caption outline thickness and shadow strength.
    26  Words at a time (1 to 6 words on screen).
    27  A see-through dark box behind the captions.
    28  Rainbow words.
    29  Extra copies in other shapes (square 1:1, landscape 16:9, 4:5) next to every clip.

    30  Watch a folder: tells you (or starts the Shorts by itself) when OBS saves a new recording.
    31  Review mode: go through new clips one by one - keep (K), delete (D), later (S / space).

  🐞 BUG HUNT - what was found and fixed
    Tested: every new feature switched on at once over 12 kinds of awkward video (no sound, one-channel sound, 24 and 60 fps, portrait, 4:3, 10-bit,
    variable frame rate, 6-second and 12-second videos, no speech), file / folder / caption text full of quotes, braces, backslashes, emoji and Japanese,
    hundreds of randomly broken settings, and the screens with empty, missing or odd folders.  Found and fixed:
     - A hand-damaged settings file (a list where a number belongs, "inf", the word "false" for a switch) could crash a render. Now repaired
       quietly and the default is used.                                                                           [affected 2.1 and older]
     - "Compare 3 looks" crashed if the Tools window had been opened and closed before.
     - Open windows (Tools, Review, Compare) kept the OLD colours when you changed the theme. They now follow it.
     - A first version of the settings fix stopped the "reaction words" from ever reaching the video. Caught by the old tests; fixed.
     - Reaction words could have been a picture-less emoji box on a PC without an emoji font; they are plain words now.
     - The Health, Clean up, Space and My words pages came back empty because a background job touched the screen. Fixed.
     - A cut-off look code was accepted and half-applied. It is now refused with a plain message.
     - Singing was heard in some speech and sirens in some music (false alarms); the detector is stricter now.
     - Several ffmpeg details (fade lengths, the caption box layer, the order of audio filters) corrected before release.
     - Times typed as 1:20:00, 83:20, 90 or 1h2m3s are all understood; a bad time is explained instead of ignored.
    Not a bug, but good to know: a video with no sound track can't be transcribed, and a video shorter than one clip can't be cut. The app says so in
    plain words and does not crash.

  What 2.2 does NOT do (on purpose)
    - It does not post for you, show followers / views / analytics, or track your face - those need your own logins / developer keys or a camera model
      that a portable program cannot safely carry. The planner still reminds you; you press upload.
    - It makes no pictures from scratch.
    - It is not a trained model. See "How trained is meant here" above.

FROM 1.9 (beta)
  NEW: 🎭 Chibi emotes for story time   (Shorts / Recap -> the "Emotes" tab)
    Pictures of your model (made with EmoteLab or anything else) pop up while you tell a story, matching the feeling of what you are
    saying at that moment: scared when you say "I was so scared", laughing on "hahaha", dead on "I'm dead", a blank stare when you go quiet.
    They pop in with a little motion (shake for fear / angry, bounce for laughing / celebrating, jump for shocked, sink for dead, sway for
    tired / crying, slide for cool, creep for yandere) and fade out again. Switch it on with "Show my chibi emotes while I tell a story".

    HOW THE PROGRAM KNOWS WHEN TO USE THEM
      - It reads the words of the clip (the same words used for the captions), one sentence at a time, and looks for the feeling in it.
        It knows about 1,500 words and phrases for 27 feelings: strong ones ("terrified", "I'm dead", "lets go", "crying laughing"),
        normal ones ("scary", "annoying", "winner") and mild ones ("worried", "weird"). A mild hint alone is not enough on "Balanced",
        two of them in one sentence are.
      - It understands "not": "I was not scared" and "it wasn't funny" show nothing. "so scared", "really mad" count stronger.
      - A "!" at the end and a loud moment in the audio make fear / angry / shocked / celebrating stronger. "hahaha", "lol", "kekw" and
        "lololol" are laughing. A short "what?" is confused. A silence of 1.4 seconds or more in the middle of talking is a blank stare.
      - Not every sentence gets a picture: it picks the strongest moments, keeps a gap between pictures (3 s on Balanced), never shows two at
        once, does not repeat the same feeling again and again, and takes turns when you have several pictures for one feeling.
      - "How many": Only the big moments (about 3 a minute) / Balanced (about 6) / Lots of reactions (about 10).
      - It never needs the internet and nothing is sent anywhere. It works from word lists, not from an AI that understands the whole
        story - so sarcasm and unusual words can fool it. That is why you can look at the plan first:
    ✎ CHECK THE PLAN FIRST   (tick "Let me check and change the emote plan before the videos render")
      Before the videos are made, a window lists every picture: the second, the feeling, the picture and WHAT WAS SAID that made the app
      choose it. Click a line to change the feeling or picture, type a new time, make it stay longer or shorter, remove it, add one, or go
      "Back to the app's plan". "No emotes this time" and "Skip checking" are there too. (Your VOD is never changed.)
    🖼 MY EMOTES   (button in the Emotes tab)
      - (Since 2.1 the program has no pictures of its own - you add yours. Before 2.1 there were 16 starter pictures.)
      - "Add pictures..." copies your PNG / WebP / GIF files into C:\Users\<you>\VTuberClipper Emotes (a see-through PNG looks best).
        The feeling is guessed from the file name (my_cry_2.png = Crying); you can also drop files into that folder yourself.
      - "Feelings..." on a picture: tick every feeling it is for (one picture can serve several). Several pictures for one feeling
        take turns.
      - "My own words": words or short phrases that should bring a feeling up for YOU ("skill issue, big yikes"). They count as strong hints.
      - "New feeling...": your own feelings (Facepalm, Evil laugh, Hungry...) with their own words.
      - "Try a sentence": type something you might say and see which feeling the app hears, because of which word, and which picture it
        would show. It also tells you when a hint is too weak for your "How many" setting.
    Good to know
      - When you have no picture for a feeling, the app tells you ("I also heard 3x Confused - add a picture for that feeling"), and the
        plan window shows it too. Add one in My emotes and it is used from then on.
      - The feeling of a picture is guessed from its file name. If one is wrong, press "Feelings..." on it and change it.
      - Emotes sit left / right (taking turns, or always one side), or on top - set under "Where" - above the captions, inside the
        TikTok / Shorts safe area. "Size" is a % of the video width.
      - "Also react to loud moments when nobody said a feeling" is for gameplay VODs (a shocked / laughing / celebrating reaction on loud peaks).
      - "Use them in Recap videos too" and "Put the clip's strongest feeling on the cover picture" are in the same tab.
      - The little emoji pictures above the captions pause while a chibi is showing, so the same feeling is never shown twice.
      - The style preview (Effects / Captions tabs) shows the emotes on its demo story.
      - Your pictures stay on your PC in C:\Users\<you>\VTuberClipper Emotes (emotes.json there remembers feelings and words).

HOW THE LOOKS WERE CHOSEN
  They follow what VTuber clip editors and Opus Clip / StreamLadder users ask for most: the model cam on top with the
  game below, big word-by-word captions with the spoken word highlighted, a hook line in the first second, a quick
  zoom-in on every reaction, a short sound hit on the big moments, silences cut, and a clip that ends on a finished
  sentence. Story streams get slower pacing and full-sentence captions; gameplay streams get faster cuts and louder hits.

SETTINGS (top right)
  Theme        18 themes (pastel, dark, VTuber and two in your own colours) and a chibi-you banner mascot (plain, cat, bunny, fox, witch, angel, devil, robot, ghost).
  Output       Quality  Extreme 135 / High 80 / Medium 50 / Low 25 Mbps
               Resolution 720p / 1080p / 1440p / 4K (the SHORT side of the video)      Framerate 25 / 30 / 60 fps
               (bitrate is a target - simple scenes can come out smaller; going above your VOD's own
               resolution only enlarges it)
  Downloads    cookies, downloader auto-update.
  Performance  save folder, speech model, speech accuracy, processor, clips rendered at once, GPU video encoding (NVENC / AMF / QuickSync),
               and "Install GPU speech pack" (NVIDIA only, optional, ~1.4 GB one time download).
  Tools        back up / restore all your settings, "Your numbers", mascot tips on / off, save or copy the Details log.

Files are saved in <save folder>\Shorts (or Shorts\<video name> with "own folder"), \Recaps, \Twitter, \PostKits, \Moments and \VODs.
The first transcription downloads the speech model (~460 MB for "small"; internet needed once).
Transcripts are saved next to the video (.words.json; other languages / hints get their own file) so scanning,
shorts and recaps of the same VOD are fast. The audio analysis is saved in %USERPROFILE%\.vtuberclipper_cache
(it is safe to delete that folder).
Time estimates learn from your PC and get better after each job.
If something ever goes wrong, press "Details" in the app. A crash log is written to
%USERPROFILE%\VTuberClipper_log.txt.
Your favourites and posted marks are kept in %USERPROFILE%\.vtuberclipper.json together with your settings. Your saved styles live in %USERPROFILE%\.vtuberclipper_presets.json, previews in %USERPROFILE%\.vtuberclipper_previews,
profiles in %USERPROFILE%\.vtuberclipper_profiles.json.

GOOD TO KNOW
  * "Find it for me" is a best guess: it looks for a box in the picture whose border stays put while the game moves. A
    rectangular minimap can fool it - that's why you always get to check and adjust the box before it is used.
  * Drag-and-drop of a video onto the window needs Windows; if it ever doesn't react, use "Choose video…".

Not included (yet): scheduling / auto-posting to social sites, telling several speakers apart,
game-specific moment detection (it listens for loud / excited moments instead). Hashtags are good evergreen picks for each app,
not live trend data. Finding games is a best guess from the picture, breaks and what you say - check the list before you build.
