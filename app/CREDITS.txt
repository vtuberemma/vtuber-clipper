VTuber Clipper - sound credits
==============================

The sound packs built into the app are CC0 ("public domain"): you may use them in any video,
on any platform, also for money, and you do not have to give credit. The credits below
are a thank-you, not a requirement.

The app has NO background music of its own. See the last part of this file.

MUSIC YOU ADD YOURSELF
----------------------
Background music comes only from songs you add: "Add songs..." copies them into the folder
"VTuberClipper Music" in your user folder (or point the app at any other song or folder).
The app cannot know whether you may use a song. Game, TV and film soundtracks and most chart music
are claimed by their owners on YouTube, TikTok and Twitch, which can mute, demonetise or remove
your video. Songs under Creative Commons "Attribution" (CC BY) need a credit in your video description.
Only use music you made, bought a licence for, or that is public domain / CC0.


CHIBI EMOTES
------------
The 16 starter pictures (app\emotes) and the chibi banner mascots (app\mascot\chibi_*) are made from the chibi pictures of your own model that
you made with EmoteLab. They are yours, not CC0, and they stay on your PC. Pictures you add yourself are copied into the folder
"VTuberClipper Emotes" in your user folder and are only used by this app. If you ever share this program with someone else, take the
pictures out first, and check the terms of the tool or artist behind any picture before you use it in monetised videos.


NEW IN 2.0
----------
All the new sounds (the six bleep sounds, the emote pop sounds, the voice effects) are made by the program itself from plain tones and filters
while your video is rendered - no sound files from anywhere are shipped for them, so there is nothing to credit.
Your chibi pictures are yours (see CHIBI EMOTES).


CLIP-FINDING ADVICE  (Smart finding, new in 2.2)
------------------------------------------------
Smart finding is not a trained model. Its word lists, sound detectors and clip lengths are rules of thumb. They were tuned on sounds made by the app's test tools
and on published advice about short videos: articles and analyses by Opus (opus.pro), Subscribr, EasyViral, Virlo and Streams Charts about how long a short should
be, what to put in the first seconds and how streamers' clips spread, and write-ups by Sportskeeda and Know Your Meme about well-known VTuber moments.
Those sources' claims were not independently verified. No VTuber's video, voice or model was copied or used for training.


SAFETY CHECKS AND LIMITS  (new in 2.3)
--------------------------------------
The flash safety check counts fast brightness changes in the finished picture and warns above three flashes in any second. That limit is the common rule of thumb
from the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG 2.3.1, "Three Flashes or Below Threshold") and from the broadcast guides it comes from (Ofcom, ITU). The program
measures the picture itself; it is a guide, not a medical test, and I could not check it against a certified tester.
The length limits and text limits used for TikTok, YouTube Shorts, Instagram Reels and X are the ones I know of as of 2025. The apps change them now and then.
The update checker asks GitHub's public releases page (api.github.com, repository vtuberemma/vtuber-clipper) for the newest release number. Nothing else is sent.
The privacy guard, the clue finders and the quality checks are rules written for this program; no outside model, service or data set is used.
